Volume II — Logistics Network &amp; Production Chain
v0.1 — April 2026
This document covers the Logistics Network and the first complete production chain. It assumes the Building System and Power Network from Volume I are implemented and working. Read alongside the Requirements Document.
1. Document Overview
Volume II covers the two systems that transform a powered building site into a working factory: the Logistics Network, which moves items between machines, and the Production Chain, which defines what those machines make. Together these systems complete the core automation loop. Once Volume II is implemented the player can mine ore, smelt it into plates, assemble plates into components, and have every step happen automatically without manual intervention.

2. Resolved Design Decisions
The following questions were open in the requirements document or arose naturally from Volume I design. They are answered here before the detailed sections begin.
✅  Decision — Item transport model:  Discrete tick-based conveyor belts for all solid items in V1. Each belt segment is one grid cell wide and moves items at a defined rate. Fluid pipes are deferred — oil and similar resources will be introduced in a later volume when the pipe system is designed. This keeps V1 logistics simple and focused on the solid-item production chain.
✅  Decision — Routing behaviour at splits:  Round-robin distribution. When a conveyor feeds into a splitter with multiple outputs, items alternate between available outputs evenly. If one output is backed up, items continue down the other outputs rather than blocking the belt. This is predictable, easy to reason about, and avoids the complexity of demand-aware routing in V1.
✅  Decision — Machine idle behaviour:  Machines stall in place when blocked. If a machine&apos;s output is full and cannot push items forward, the machine stops its production cycle and waits. It does not discard items or continue producing. Power continues to be drawn at idle rate (25% of operating draw) while stalled. This creates meaningful backpressure that teaches the player to balance throughput.
✅  Decision — Progression model for V1:  Production milestones. Tier 2 unlocks automatically when the player has cumulatively produced defined quantities of Tier 1 items. No separate research infrastructure is required. The UI shows a progress bar toward the next tier so the player always knows how close they are. This is the simplest model that still creates a sense of industrial growth.
✅  Decision — Manual crafting interface:  The player can open a crafting panel from their inventory screen at any time. Manual crafting uses items from the personal inventory and produces directly into it. It is available throughout the game but capped at one item per craft action — no bulk manual crafting. This ensures manual crafting remains useful for one-off needs without replacing automation.
3. Logistics Network
3.1 Overview
The Logistics Network is the system of conveyors, inserters, and splitters that moves items between machines and storage within a construct. It is the circulatory system of the factory. Without it, machines produce in isolation and the player must manually carry every item.
The logistics network is entirely passive from the power network&apos;s perspective. Conveyor belts and inserters do not consume power in V1. This is a deliberate simplification — it removes one balancing concern from early factory design and lets the player focus on item flow layout rather than power budgeting for infrastructure.
📝  Design Note:  This can be revisited post-V1. Adding power costs to conveyors and inserters would create a meaningful trade-off between logistics density and power capacity, which becomes interesting at larger factory scales.
3.2 Conveyor Belts
Conveyor belts are the primary item transport component. A belt occupies one grid cell and has a defined facing direction. Items placed on the belt travel in the facing direction at a fixed rate. Belts connect end-to-end when placed in a continuous line. A belt that faces into the side of another belt merges its items onto that belt.
✅  Decision — Belt simulation model:  Slot-based. Each belt cell has two item slots (left lane and right lane). Each tick, items in each slot attempt to advance one position forward. If the next slot is occupied, the item waits. This model is simple, deterministic, and visually clear — the player can always see exactly where items are and why they are not moving.
Belt throughput is expressed as items per second per lane. The V1 belt tier moves 1 item per second per lane, giving a maximum of 2 items per second per belt segment. A faster belt tier is available at Tier 2 and doubles throughput.

3.3 Inserters
Inserters are single-cell components that pick items from one location and place them in another. They bridge the gap between belt lanes and machine input/output slots. Without inserters, belts and machines cannot exchange items — an inserter is always required to load a machine from a belt or unload a machine onto a belt.
✅  Decision — Inserter operation model:  Arm-swing with configurable item filter. An inserter has a source cell and a target cell (the two cells it faces across). Each inserter cycle it picks one item from the source, holds it briefly, then deposits it in the target. Cycle time is fixed per inserter tier. The player can optionally set an item filter on the inserter so it only picks items of a specific type, ignoring others. No filter means the inserter takes whatever is available.

3.4 Splitters
Splitters are 1×2-cell components that accept items from one belt input and distribute them across two belt outputs. They are the primary tool for dividing item streams to feed multiple machines from a single source belt.
✅  Decision — Splitter distribution:  Round-robin per lane. Each output belt receives items alternately. A splitter can optionally be configured with a priority output — items go to the priority side first and only overflow to the secondary side. Priority mode is useful for keeping a critical machine fed before routing surplus elsewhere. This is a single toggle, not a full routing rule system.
Splitters can also merge two input belts into one output, functioning as a merger when items enter from both sides. The merge uses the same round-robin logic — one item from each input alternates onto the output.
3.5 Storage Chests
Storage chests are passive containers that hold items and make them available to adjacent inserters. They do not pull or push items themselves — they only accept or provide items when an inserter acts on them. Chests are the primary buffer point in a production chain.

3.6 Item Flow Rules
The following rules govern how items move through the logistics network and interact with machines. These rules must be implemented consistently to prevent edge cases that break production chains.
Backpressure
If a machine&apos;s output slot is full and the inserter or belt taking from it is also full, the machine stalls. It does not discard outputs. The stall propagates backward through the belt until it reaches a machine or storage that can absorb the excess. This is intentional and teaches the player to balance production rates and throughput.
Insertion Priority
When a machine has multiple input types and multiple inserters feeding it, there is no coordination between inserters — each inserter acts independently. If both inputs are available, both inserters fire on their own cycle. The machine begins its production cycle only when all required inputs are present in its internal buffer, regardless of which inserter delivered them first.
Belt Compression
A belt is considered &quot;compressed&quot; when every slot is occupied. A compressed belt at the input side of a machine is the most efficient delivery state. Gaps in a belt reduce effective throughput. Players should be able to observe belt compression visually — a compressed belt looks visually full, a sparse belt has visible gaps between items.
Inter-Construct Logistics
Logistics networks do not automatically span separate constructs. Items cannot travel from one construct to another via a belt unless the two constructs are physically connected and share the same construct membership. A gap of one cell between two belt lines breaks the connection. This forces the player to either connect constructs or use vehicles for inter-base item transfer in V1.

4.2 Miner
The miner is a specialised machine that extracts raw resources from resource nodes. It has no recipe input — it produces continuously as long as it is powered and the resource node beneath it has remaining yield.
Placement rule: a miner must be placed so that its extraction footprint covers at least one resource node cell. Miners placed away from a node are invalid and cannot be confirmed.

4.3 Electric Furnace
The electric furnace smelts raw ores into plates. It is the first processing machine in every production chain and acts as the gateway from raw resources to usable materials.
The furnace has one input slot (ore), one output slot (plates), and draws power while operating. It executes smelting recipes automatically — there is no manual recipe selection required. The furnace detects the ore type in its input slot and applies the matching smelt recipe.
📝  Design Note:  Auto-detecting the recipe based on input type is a deliberate simplification for furnaces. It avoids the player needing to configure each furnace individually. Assemblers, by contrast, require explicit recipe selection because they can execute many different recipes.

4.4 Assembler Mk1
The assembler is the core manufacturing machine. Unlike the furnace, the assembler requires the player to explicitly select a recipe. It can execute any recipe in the &quot;basic&quot; recipe category. It has multiple input slots and one output slot.
The player selects a recipe by opening the assembler&apos;s interface panel and choosing from the list of available recipes. Once a recipe is set, the assembler will only consume and produce items matching that recipe. Changing the recipe clears the machine&apos;s internal item buffers — any items already inside are ejected onto adjacent belts or lost if no belt is present.
❓  Open — Recipe change item fate:  Should items in a machine&apos;s buffer be ejected onto an adjacent belt when the recipe is changed, or should they be returned to the player&apos;s inventory, or simply lost? Ejecting to a belt is most forgiving but requires the belt to have space. Recommendation: attempt to eject to any connected output belt; if no belt has space, return to player inventory; if inventory is also full, the recipe change is blocked until space is made.

7.3 Tier Progress UI
The current tier progress must always be visible to the player without opening a menu. A compact progress indicator in the HUD shows the current milestone item, the current count, and the target count (e.g. &quot;Circuit Boards: 47 / 100&quot;). When the milestone is reached a prominent unlock notification appears and a brief visual flourish plays.
The player should be able to open a full progression screen that shows all tiers, their unlock conditions, and what each tier adds. Locked tiers should show their content as a preview so the player always knows what they are working toward.

8.2 Key Technical Risks
Belt item simulation at scale — slot-based belts are simple per-cell but can be expensive when thousands of cells are ticking every frame. Consider spatial partitioning to only tick belt segments that have items on them. Empty belt sections should not tick.
Inserter reach across construct boundaries — an inserter placed at the edge of a construct must not be able to reach into a different construct&apos;s machine. Validate both source and target cells against the inserter&apos;s owning construct at the moment of placement.
Recipe change clearing machine buffers — clearing internal buffers on recipe change is straightforward but must handle the case where the output belt is full and the machine can&apos;t eject. Define a clear fallback (block the change, or send to player inventory) early to avoid an unresolved state mid-game.
Milestone tracking across sessions — the Circuit Board count must persist correctly in the save file even if the player has already consumed some boards in construction. The tracker counts cumulative production, not current inventory.
